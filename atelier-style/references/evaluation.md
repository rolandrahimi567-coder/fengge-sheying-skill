# Comparison and decision evidence

Follow the user's agreed call budget and design. Freeze exact briefs, prompts, skill files and evaluation criteria before generation. Keep the name-only competitor. To diagnose suspected overconstraint, remove one factor at a time; if gesture and framing both differ, a 2x2 ablation plus name baseline can distinguish those hypotheses. Do not simultaneously add softness or a new style vocabulary and attribute the result to the deleted gesture.

Use the same available image generator and reference policy in every arm. Preserve all first outcomes, including failures. No refusal rewriting, replacement draws or result cherry-picking. Record exact tool arguments and available model/usage/cost; unknown seed or model versions limit reproducibility. Separate calls do not prove statistically independent samples.

Inspect original brief fulfillment independently from added-rule execution. Use pairwise preference for overall image and target direction: left, right, tie or uncertain, with a concrete visual reason. Also describe physical artifacts, partial rule execution and repeated staging. Do not count a hand touching a collar, extra texture or more blur as automatic style success.

Hide method labels and keep a reveal record for later reviewers. An agent that constructed prompts or saw the mapping is an informed reviewer even when labels are hidden. Do not prefill human approval. Keep explicit user feedback separate from assistant observations and source attribution.

An already-used topic is diagnostic, not held-out validation. When a change looks promising, freeze a candidate and evaluate fresh topics against both the prior version and name-only prompting. Test other branches if shared compilation changes. Improvement over the old version alone is not proof of value over the name baseline. Small repeated sets identify issues, not stable success rates or statistical superiority.

Photographer recognition requires authenticated representative work and appropriate alternative-author comparisons. Without those, report target-direction alignment as provisional. Do not claim that corpus rule execution certifies attribution, or that a prompt-level ablation validates a substantially different selective compiler. Record what was actually tested and leave unsupported claims unknown.
