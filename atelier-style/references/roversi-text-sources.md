# Roversi: sourced concepts and conditional applications

Research supplement to 3.1, checked 2026-09-10. These are curated paraphrases, not quotations, visual measurements, training data or proven generation improvements. Read the applicable card only. Preserve the user's complete brief and chosen photographer; adding nothing remains a valid decision.

## How to use

1. Identify a concrete unresolved decision in this brief. Select a relevant card, or none.
2. Distinguish source claim, your interpretation and the proposed image instruction. An artist's account of a live shoot does not establish how an image model responds to words.
3. If the brief already expresses the desired relationship, do not repeat it. When guidance is justified, place one concise application in an appropriate existing optional decision field and explain its use in `guidance_reasons` and `selection_reason`. Mention the card ID there for traceability; do not put RC IDs in `applied_relations` or pretend they were validated against corpus images.
4. Keep citations and research reasoning in the decision record, outside the generation prompt. Do not append all three cards automatically. Review resulting images against name-only results when claiming an improvement.

## RC01 — Person and clothes both contribute to the portrait

- **Source:** S1, Roversi's interview response: he understands fashion photographs as portraits of the person and their clothes together.
- **Interpretation:** A garment need not become an isolated product display; the person and its form can contribute together. This does not determine their exact area ratio.
- **When useful:** The brief asks for a face–garment relationship but a planned setup separates the two or adds a gratuitous garment-display gesture.
- **Possible application, not an author quotation:** Let the person's presence and the garment's volume contribute together to the portrait.
- **Leave open:** Hand position, camera distance, fabric, color and focus hierarchy unless required by this image.
- **Failure to watch:** Making the clothing huge, or repeating a collar-touch gesture, without strengthening the portrait. More cloth is not evidence of a better relationship.

## RC02 — Reduce habitual performance without erasing expression

- **Source:** S2, Roversi describes a subtractive approach to conventional expressive masks in a discussion of relaxed portrait subjects. It is a situated account of working with people, not a rule for every fashion image.
- **Interpretation:** For an explicitly quiet portrait, reduce conspicuous posing while keeping an individual presence.
- **When useful:** An added action makes a restrained request look demonstrative. If the user already says quiet and restrained, extra wording may be redundant.
- **Possible application, not an author quotation:** Use a quiet, unforced expression; let posture respond to the garment without an emphatic display gesture.
- **Leave open:** Eye direction and degree of expression. Do not translate subtraction into blankness, universal sadness, closed eyes, undressing or compulsory blur.
- **Failure to watch:** Repeated lifeless faces or identical passive poses. Subtlety must not erase the person.

## RC03 — Preserve room for unplanned choices

- **Source:** S2 describes openness to chance when an initial idea meets a real shoot; S3 discusses chance, concentration and an intense encounter. These describe photographic practice.
- **Interpretation:** During prompt planning, question staging choices that serve no requirement. This is an assistant's transfer hypothesis, not evidence that the word spontaneity produces spontaneity.
- **When useful:** The plan specifies exact hands, crop, background and lighting merely to fill all fields.
- **Application:** Omit unjustified optional fields. Usually this changes the decision process rather than adding a sentence to the image prompt.
- **Leave open:** Only unspecified choices. Explicit user requirements remain binding.
- **Failure to watch:** Ignoring requirements in the name of chance, or mistaking random output variation for creative intent.

## Source register and limits

- **S1 — AnOther / Miss Rosen, 2019-09-23.** [Paolo Roversi interview](https://www.anothermag.com/art-photography/11951/paolo-roversi-interview-fashion-photography-intangible-presence-fahey-klein). Read interview text; primary evidence for his stated double-portrait idea. Editorial framing is separate from his responses.
- **S2 — The Talks, 2013-02-18.** [Paolo Roversi interview](https://the-talks.com/interview/paolo-roversi/). Read interview text; primary evidence for his stated approach to expression and chance. Historical context; equipment remarks are not a current equipment inventory.
- **S3 — Vogue Italia / Chiara Barzini, 2017-11-08.** [Intervista a Paolo Roversi](https://www.vogue.it/news/vogue-arte/2017/11/08/intervista-paolo-roversi-vogue-italia-novembre-2017). Read Italian interview text; supports his account of encounter and chance. Translations and operational applications are interpretations.
- **S4 — Palais Galliera, 2024 exhibition.** [Official exhibition overview](https://www.palaisgalliera.paris.fr/expositions/paolo-roversi-0). Read institutional overview. It describes variety across a long career, including different treatments of light and color; this does not justify one compulsory palette or focus treatment. The artist participated in exhibition direction, so this is not wholly independent criticism.
- **S5 — Palais Galliera, exhibition catalogue.** [Official publication page](https://www.palaisgalliera.paris.fr/expositions/publications/paolo-roversi). Read bibliographic metadata and contents only, not the book. Useful for locating further research; not evidence that its chapters have been analyzed.

Artist testimony supports what an artist says about their practice; curatorial authority provides context. Neither establishes a uniquely identifying visual feature or proves that a generation instruction works. Repeated reporting of the same exhibition or interview is not independent confirmation. These sources do not meet a universal superlative such as the most objective text.

## Initial pilot status, 2026-09-10

One familiar adult-woman/white-sculptural-gown brief; name-only, conceptual-summary and operational-translation arms, initially two draws per arm. Five calls produced four images and one provider safety rejection; the duplicate summary prompt was skipped without retry. The summary arm has no image evidence. Name-only and translated guidance each have two images, no controlled seed, no independent blind or human review and no separate photographer-recognition panel. The assistant's informed review found no clear translated-guidance advantage; both arms showed similar portrait conventions. The three cards remain conditional research support, not an automatically appended prompt package. This pilot neither proves superiority nor establishes that the concepts are useless in other tasks.
